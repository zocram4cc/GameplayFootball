# 29 August 2026 — showcase defect sweep

Seven defects reported against a full-length real-time DBG v SMBG capture
(`/home/z/.claude/jobs/f858a344/tmp/full_dbg_smb.mp4`, taken after the
framerate/replay-freeze/anim-bucket fixes of the same day). Each was located
with a timestamp; evidence and root cause recorded below, in report order.

## Fixed

### 1. Arms break / wrists invert the instant a celebration starts (04:34, 10:25, 29:56)

**Root cause: `GoalCelebration::LoopVariable` invents a loop pairing that does
not exist for PES's numbered celebration library.**

`goal_cutscenes.py`'s `assign_variables` numbers every *filmed* celebration
sequentially (100, 101, 102...) in alphabetical order of its name -
`celebrate_0057` got var 146 because it is the 47th name alphabetically, not
because 146 means anything about the clip itself. `GoalCelebration::LoopVariable`
then assumes `var + 10` is *this clip's own held loop* and asks
`AnimCollection::HasSpecial` whether one is installed. For the numbered pool -
213 of roughly 230 celebrations, the overwhelming majority - it almost always
finds *something* at that number, because vars are dense: 146 + 10 = 156, which
is `celebrate_0069`, a completely unrelated celebration.

Rendered both offline with `pose_render.py` (linear-blend skin, no engine, no
blending) to settle whether this was a data problem or an engine problem:
`celebrate_0057` skins clean across 13 sampled frames (a legitimate run then
supine "fall and roll" performance - the "buu, tien & co. float" report at
08:40 turned out to be this same clip, correctly authored). `celebrate_0069`
shows **two overlapping heads and two sets of raised arms in one render** -
it is a two-actor PES clip whose conversion put both actors' curves on a
single skeleton. The moment `goalCelebrationIntroHold_ms` (celebrate_0057's own
~3.2 s) elapsed, the scorer was handed this clip instead - a stranger's
two-body performance overlaid on his one skeleton, which is the broken arms
and inverted wrists on screen.

Confirmed the mechanism is scorer-specific: teammates' generic mood reaction
("happy_normal"/"happy_extreme", `madeGoal` 1 or 2) is a different, hand-curated
install path where a handful of clips really do ship a same-named `_loop`
suffix pair (e.g. `pes_dm_goal_archer.anim` var 1, `pes_dm_goal_archer_loop.anim`
var 11) - legitimate, and left untouched.

**Fix**: `src/onthepitch/match.cpp` no longer asks `CelebrationClipFrames` about
`chosen.var + 10` (`loopFrames` is always 0 for the scorer's own performance
length now); `src/onthepitch/player/controller/elizacontroller.cpp` gates
`loopExists` on `!isScorer`, so the scorer's celebration is asked for by its
own `filmed` var for its whole length and never hands off to whatever
unrelated clip happens to share that number plus ten.

### 2. Two linesmen still visibly jogging through an offside/foul hold (15:23, 19:30)

**Root cause: the linesman AI never checked whether play was stopped.**
`RefereeController::RequestCommand`'s referee case branches on
`match->GetReferee()->GetBuffer().active` to switch from chasing the offender
to a static reaction pose; the linesman case had no equivalent branch at all -
it issued a fresh `e_FunctionType_Movement` command toward the live offside
line every single tick, cutscene or not. A camera holding on him for a
reaction shot caught him mid-stride because he never stopped moving, which
also reads as "missing arms" when a running pose is held on a frame with the
arm swung back out of a shot expecting a static stance. The two-linesman
overlap at 15:23 is very likely a side effect of the same defect (nothing
ever stopped either man's chase toward the same offside line while both were
close to it).

**Fix**: `src/onthepitch/player/controller/refereecontroller.cpp`'s linesman
case now holds current position and facing (zero velocity, look along his own
direction) whenever `!match->IsInPlay()`, mirroring the referee's own
stoppage-aware branch.

## Still open

### 3. Background players occasionally "levitate" horizontally near a celebration (08:40)

Confirmed this specific report is `celebrate_0057` itself, seen correctly -
the offline render shows a legitimate authored dive-and-roll. Not chased
further as a defect; kept open only because the *loop-mismatch* mechanism in
#1 was proven real for the scorer, and the same manifest quirk (many intros
sharing one shared mood var, one shared loop var) means a **teammate's**
`madeGoal` reaction could in principle be handed a loop clip that does not
match the intro he was just playing, inside the happy_normal/happy_extreme
family. Not fixed because it was not directly observed broken - `!isScorer`
loop lookups were left exactly as they were. Re-check against the
re-verification capture below before spending more time on it.

### 4. The whole showcase runs slower than its configured real-time factor

Measured against this capture, not guessed: at `match_duration_minutes 20`
(factor 90/20 = 4.5 game-ms per real ms), the first half took 850 real
seconds to cover a 2558-game-second clock advance (67% of the configured
rate); the second half took 624 real seconds for 2508 game-seconds (89%).
Not a uniform clock-formula bug - `MatchDurationGameTimeFromRealMilliseconds`
is correct, and the "game" `TaskSequence` (`src/main.cpp:571`) is registered
non-skippable, so `Scheduler::Run`'s absolute-time catch-up (`framework/scheduler.cpp`)
already replays every tick a slow render frame delayed rather than dropping
it - confirmed no catch-up cap exists, and the two halves recovering
differently (67% -> 89%) rules out an unbounded "spiral of death" (that would
only ever get worse). What is left is plain CPU throughput: the "game"
sequence and the "graphics" sequence compete for the same core(s), and 44+
imported high-poly player models (raised from a 20,000 to a 100,000 triangle
budget for #38) plus a full stadium crowd cannot sustain the intended 100 Hz
tick alongside rendering during the heaviest scenes (dense cutscene shots,
several clustered characters). Fixing the rate requires reducing per-tick or
per-frame cost (LOD on imported models, cheaper crowd instancing, or genuine
multi-core offload of skinning) - a performance project, not a bug fix, and
out of scope for this pass. Documented rather than patched so it is not
re-litigated as a scheduler bug.

### 5. Half-time menu shows a near-empty pitch instead of a kickoff lineup (18:42)

Checked `Match::SetMatchPhase`: `ResetSituation` + `referee->PrepareSetPiece`
only fire when the phase actually flips to `e_MatchPhase_2ndHalf`, which - by
design ("Snap players to restart positions instantly under the wipe",
committed earlier) - happens under the wipe *after* the "BEGIN SECOND HALF"
menu closes, not while it is showing. The screenshot at 18:42 is the menu's
first frame, before that flip: what is behind it is deliberately last
frame of the first half (mostly benched/scattered), not a lineup. Followed
the actual resume forward: by clock 47:51 (video ~19:30, well after the menu
closed) play is ordinary and correctly formed. No corroborating evidence of
an actual defect once the pre-wipe/post-wipe distinction is accounted for;
left unfixed for want of a real repro. Worth another look only with a
screenshot taken at the wipe-out moment itself, not the menu's opening frame.
