### Keeper: the lateral gap is the bisector's own floor, not the lookahead

Lateral is repeatable (1.99 m) and overhead is shot variety (0.00-11.13 m).
The first reading of that was a positioning bias caused by the 667 ms
lookahead, and `023e6a3` shipped a clamp for it. **That was wrong, and the
commit is reverted.** Modelling the keeper's geometry offline (the engine's
bisector-from-predicted-ball, intersected with the backline and clamped to the
posts) says otherwise:

| bisector computed from | mean lateral error | max |
|---|---:|---:|
| the CURRENT ball (zero lookahead) | 1.64 m | 2.60 m |
| the full 667 ms reading | 1.69 m | 2.74 m |
| the clamped reading | 1.69 m | 2.73 m |

The lookahead is worth 0.05 m. **~1.6 m is the floor of angle-bisector
positioning itself**, and the measured 1.99 m is that floor plus the slack of
him not arriving where he aimed. The addressing headroom on the lateral axis is
therefore ~0.3 m, not 2 m - and no lookahead, clamp or come-out tuning can
recover it. Do not re-derive a positioning fix from this number.

What the diagnostic does say is that the keeper's problem is **timing**:

- **19 of 44 beaten keepers (43%) were out of TIME** - the ball reached his
  plane inside his own reaction, so `KeeperEffectiveReach_m` granted no dive
  at all. Reach and position cannot help those.
- `keeperOutOfTime_s` is 0.25 s and his reaction at gk_reflexes 0.734 is
  0.24 s, so "out of time" is by construction "arrived before he could move".

Next lever is the timing path (`GetKeeperReactionTime_s`, and what decides
that a shot is closeable at all) - and deliberately small, because the batch
noise is +-2 goals/match.

KEPT from `023e6a3`: the `simbatch.sh` pass-fail capture (below). REVERTED:
the clamp, its test, and the wiring.

### The trap cliff is dead; the keeper is a reaction problem (24-09, 2 matches)

Two matches, concurrency 2, bar 0.18, seeds 5000-5001, with the corrected trap
census and the pace counter:

```
[balance-passing]   accuracy 65/62 | 69/65   clean 50/52 | 56/49   (506 passes)
[pass-fail]         intercept 52   out 2   trap 120
[balance-passfail]  failed traps: mean incoming 10.2 m/s (n=56) and 9.3 m/s (n=64)
[balance-keeper]    out of time 8 | out of reach 10
[balance-keeper-whatif] of 18 beaten: quicker read saves 9 (50%),
                                longer standing frame saves 3 (17%)
```

**Trap is still 69% of counted failures after the instrument fix** - 120 of
174. So fixing the census did not collapse the bucket: the conclusion that
trapping is where pass accuracy is lost survives, while the earlier "70%" was
arrived at with the broken counter and should not have been quoted.

**The 28 m/s cliff hypothesis is FALSIFIED.** Failed traps arrive at 9.3-10.2
m/s - ordinary passes, a third of the speed the cliff needed. At that pace
`GetTrapKillStrength` scrubs only 12-15%, so the ball leaves the touch still
doing ~8.5 m/s, and possession needs the ball within 6.0 m/s of the receiver:

| receiver | ball-receiver difference | possession? |
|---|---:|---|
| standing (0 m/s) | 8.2-8.7 m/s | **no** |
| jogging (3.5) | 4.7-5.2 | yes |
| sprinting (8.0) | 0.2-0.7 | yes |

So a receiver who is on the spot when an ordinary pass arrives cannot take
possession of it: the pass is judged unfinished because the ball is still
moving faster than he is. That is an **approach/positioning** failure at the
reception, not a pass-weight or pace problem. `bumpyRideBias` is the term that
would explain it - a receiver arriving badly positioned keeps more of the
ball's momentum - and it is not yet recorded.

**Keeper: reaction time, not framing.** Over 18 beaten keepers a 25% quicker
read would have saved 9; a longer standing frame 3. That is the third
independent reading pointing the same way, and the first with a sample worth
quoting.

### The trap bucket and the accuracy do not draw from the same population

The same trap touch is counted in `passesCompleted` (a success,
`RecordBallTouch` at the touch, because it was his team's pass) **and** in
`AddPassFailBadTrap` (a failure, because `AI_HasPossession` was false at that
instant - usually on the 0.5 m height gate, since the mean contact is 0.63 m).
So the trap bucket was never a subset of the failed passes; it is mostly a
subset of the *completed* ones, judged too early. Quoting it as "70% of
failures" was wrong twice over - first because the stale-flag instrument
defined it wrong (`108f3dfd`), and then because even the corrected instrument
asks the question at an instant when a lofted ball cannot yet answer it.

The honest accounting from 2 matches (506 passes):

| | rate | meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| accuracy 65.0% | arrival: his team touched its own pass | the top line |
| clean 51.6% | arrival AND his team still had the last touch a window later | the quality line |
| trap 69% of "failures" | own-team trap touch with the ball above 0.5 m or beyond 1 m *at the touch* | a contact-state count, not a failure count |

So the real gap to chase is **accuracy minus clean: ~14 points of passes that
arrive and are lost within the window** - a post-reception ball-control
problem, not trapping, not pass weight, and not the 28 m/s cliff (which
never fired; failures arrive at ~10 m/s). The trap census as built cannot see
that gap, because it samples the reception instead of the window. Anything
further here starts from the clean rate, not the trap bucket.

No behaviour was changed. The instruments stay in because the next stop - the
reception window itself - needs both of them to be trusted first, and the
height gate is exactly what would have invalidated the winner.

keeper stats, the reseated XIs, the reach model, the own-goal scene - has taken
it past the mark rather than to it. xG 1.76 is now the number closest to its
target of any on the table.

Keeper: out-of-time and out-of-reach are even (45/43), where the earlier census
was 19/25 out-of-reach with a 5-7 m mean gap. The what-if over 88 beaten
keepers reads **quicker read 32% | longer standing frame 19%** - reaction time
is still the lever, now on a sample worth acting on.

Pass accuracy 65.9% is unchanged from every prior reading, which is itself the
finding: four measurement passes have not moved it, because nothing has been
changed to move it.

## Pass accuracy: a first-time pass never completed the pass it was played off

**Accounting first (`97ae4830`).** On 4 matches, intercept + out were 96 of 929
attempts (10.3%), so 89.7% reached a team, yet accuracy read 65-69%. The
buckets that could hold the other ~21 points (`[pass-ghost]`, `[pass-gk]`,
`[pass-restart]`) were compiled out of Release (`#ifndef NDEBUG`), and
`simbatch.sh` filtered them out anyway. Two ends had no counter at all: a
pending pass replaced by a new pass or clearance, and one ended by `AddShot`.
`Referee::PrepareSetPiece` also dropped the pending pass uncounted. All are
counted now, and `EveryPassAttemptEndsInExactlyOneBucket` pins completed +
intercept + out + gk + restart + shot-pending + overwritten == attempts.

Next batch (seeds 5000-5001), and the sum closes exactly: s5000 team 1 has 160
attempts = ~104 completed + 14 intercept + 1 restart + **40 overwritten** + 1
shot. Overwritten was 20-40 and 15-24 per side, i.e. **all** of the gap.

**Cause.** The pass kick (`humanoid.cpp`, the `AddPassAttempt` branch) and the
shot kick both called `SetLastTouchPlayer` but never `RecordBallTouch`. So when
the man a pass was played to sent it on first time, he opened a new attempt
over the one he had just received: neither completed nor failed. It is the
same hole the body-collision path closed for ricochets (its own comment: "cost
roughly a third of all passes"). Fix: record the kicker's touch first;
`RecordBallTouch` then books it by team, as a completion or an interception.

This is the measurement becoming right, not the passing getting better. It is
reported as such.

Overwritten went to exactly zero in both matches, which is what the fix

**The books now close: 472 attempts, 470 accounted, 2 unaccounted (0.42%).**
One per match, and the likeliest explanation is a pass still in flight at the

a fixed leak.

Clean rose with accuracy for the same reason: completions now resolve against
a correct last-touch record instead of one that had skipped a third of the
touches. Item 2 (54-72% toward ~80%) is **closed**.

## showcase.sh: the harness was silent and had no upload at all (`128c6b1c`, `9a79d52f`, `2ec3a96f`)

- **Audio.** The harness recorded no audio, so every showcase so far was a
  silent mp4. The engine mixes fine: OpenAL Soft created its context and a
  100-second match measures -42.3 dB mean, -25.9 dB max. The broken part was
  the capture. `parecord` on the default sink's monitor records **pure
  silence** on this machine - a 440 Hz tone played to that sink came back at
  ffmpeg's -91.0 dB floor, on the real sink and on a fresh null sink alike.
  A PipeWire loopback adapter with an explicit `capture.props` side works
  (same tone: -27.3 dB mean, 0.0 dB max), so that is what the harness builds,
  and the monitor route is gone rather than left failing quietly.
- "It has audio" is measured, not assumed: ffmpeg's volumedetect mean volume is
  printed on every run, and a track at or under -90 dB is dropped from the mux
  with a warning instead of shipping as a silent audio stream.
- **Upload.** None existed. catbox.moe closed anonymous uploads, but its own
  temporary host does not: litterbox, 1 GB, no account. Verified with a probe
  file - 20000 bytes up, downloaded again, identical md5. `--upload` is
  auto|account|litterbox|none; auto uses the account endpoint when
  `CATBOX_USERHASH` is set and litterbox otherwise.
- Two bugs found by running it rather than reading it: `--audio` and
  `--catbox-hash` were parsed *after* the `*)` catch-all so they had never
>  been recognised, and every validation sat at the end of the script, so
>  `--upload bogus` started the recorder and a forty-minute match before
  refusing. Validation now happens before anything starts.

## Goal rate: the keeper is not the lever, and the two targets contradict each other (`583f3476`)

**Change.** `GetKeeperReactionTime_s` 200-350 ms -> 150-262 ms, the census's own
`kWhatIfReactionScale` (0.75) applied instead of merely counted. 12 matches,
seeds 5000-5011, identical protocol to the re-baseline.

| | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| goals/match | 5.58 | **5.50** |
| saves/team | 3.7 | **4.3** |
| keeper save rate | 49% | **54%** |
| beaten out-of-reach | 43 | **34** |
| beaten out-of-time | 45 | 49 |
| pass accuracy | 66% | **83%** |

**The keeper got better and the goal rate did not move.** The change did what
the census said it would - out-of-reach beats fell by a fifth and the save
rate rose five points - and 5.58 -> 5.50 is nothing.

### Theory killed: "a keeper beaten is a goal conceded"

The census counts 83-88 beaten keepers per 12 matches against 67 goals. They
are not the same population, and that gap is the whole reason the what-if
couldn't move the number: converting 32-39% of the beaten shots converts shots
that were largely not scoring anyway. **Any further keeper tuning - reach,
dive speed, standing frame, reaction - is bounded above by this and should not
be attempted as the goal-rate lever.**

### What the shots actually do

| | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| shots/match | 17.8 | 18.5 |
| on target (projected) | 15.2 | 15.8 |
| saves/match | 7.4 | 8.6 |
| goals/match | 5.58 | 5.50 |
| misses: wide / over / short | 2.5 / 0.0 / 0.0 | 2.7 / 0.0 / 0.0 |

**85% of every shot is on target, and 100% of the misses go wide** - not one
over the bar or short in twelve matches. The shot model has no elevation error
and no weak side: a shot either stays under the bar or drifts wide. Of what
reaches the frame, 35% goes in.

### The two targets cannot both hold

The owner's table (`simbatch.sh`: shots ~14/team, ~80% on target, goals 2-4).
Take it at its own numbers: 28 shots/match at 80% on target is 22 on target,
and 2-4 goals from that is a 9-18% conversion, so the keeper would have to
stop 82-91% of what reaches him. A real keeper stops about 70%. **At a
real-football save rate the owner's own shot target yields ~6.7 goals a match,
above the 2-4 it also asks for.**

2-4 goals instead needs ~9-12 on-target shots a match, i.e. 32-43% on target -
the real figure - against the 80% also written down. One of the two has to
give. That is the owner's call, not a tuning decision, so item 1 is recorded
as measured-and-blocked rather than closed.


## The compromise: shots 20-25, 60-70% on target, keeper a shade better (`f8d5557e`)

The owner's call on the contradiction was a compromise, and notably it drops
the explicit 2-4 goal target and keeps the other three - which makes it
self-consistent, so there is something to build.

**Shots that miss (`f8d5557e`).** The keeper batch had shown why the goal rate
would not move: every single miss went wide, none over or short. 85% of shots
on target, 15.8 of 18.5 shots reaching the frame, because the band clamps the
vertical term so a strike always crosses inside and nothing perturbed it. The
chances were never bad ones.

A strike is now aimed at a scattered point on the goal plane. The existing
projection is exactly invertible (`vz = (h - z)/t + g*t/2`), scatter is in
metres at the goal line, grows with range and shrinks with `technical_shot`.

  on target   86% -> 63%   (target 60-70%)
  misses      wide 2.7 / over 0.0 / short 0.0
            -> wide 3.8 / over 0.0 / short 2.0

**Shot volume.** Measured at the owner's own config: **36 shots/match at a 0.04
bar, 27.8 at 0.10, 15.8 at 0.18.** So the game's default (0.04) was the
outlier, not the batch harness's 0.18. Set to 0.14, the interpolation of those
three points. An n=4 run at 0.13 read 32.0 - more shots at a higher bar, which
is noise, and the reason the value comes from the three-point fit rather than
any single run: at n=4 the spread is wider than the effect.

**Keeper**, already done above: reaction 200-350 -> 150-262 ms, save rate
49% -> 54%, out-of-reach beats 43 -> 34.

Open: "over the bar" misses are still ~0-1 a match against 5-6 wide and 3-5
short. Real football misses over about as often as wide. Not asked for, and
recorded rather than fixed.
