### 1. Skin into the upload array
`GeometryData::UploadTarget`: the graphics interpreter publishes its concatenated
element-major array and per-submesh offsets; a writer that says
`SetWritesUploadTarget(true)` skins straight into it and the interpreter copies nothing
for it. The submesh arrays keep the baked bind pose (the AABB and the face rig read them).
The face rig now writes its offsets into the *bind* mesh skinning reads, under the
resource lock, instead of into skinned output the next skin overwrote.
**Regression found and fixed (5acd0af):** the target is only published for a dynamic
geometry, and the imported bodies' `fullbody.object` never said `<dynamic>` - the flag had
only ever chosen a buffer usage hint. Every imported body skinned into arrays nobody
uploaded and stood in its bind pose (`frames/grid.png`, top row: LOD off, everyone in a
T-pose; bottom row: LOD on, far bodies move, near ones do not). `HumanoidBase` now flags
its body dynamic itself and the interpreter publishes whenever the array is not the one
already published. After: `frames/grid2.png`, both rows animate.

### 2. Body LOD
`Skinning::ClusterDecimate`: vertex clustering on a 2 cm grid at load, the first vertex in
a cell stands for it (position, normal, uv and - through `sourceVertex` - the skin
weights), a triangle survives if its corners land in three cells. One `fullbody_lod`
Geometry per body under `fullbodyNode`, enabled/disabled against the full one with 2 m of
hysteresis at `body_lod_distance` (30 m; 0 disables; `body_lod_cell` 0.02). The copy
coming on is skinned the same frame (`bodyLodSwitched`). 1.39 M skinned vertices a
frame -> 165 k when everyone is far; kits follow on `SetKit`. Tests:
`tests/onthepitch/skinning_lod_test.cpp`.
Frames: `frames/close_lodon/shot_20000.png` (celebration, five bodies within 5 m, full
detail); `frames/far_ab.png` (left LOD off, right LOD on, bodies ~35 m, silhouettes and
kits intact, no holes); `frames/grid2.png` (no doubled body in any frame). The "lossless
up close" claim is structural as well as visual: inside 28 m the identical full geometry
object is what is drawn.

### 3. Skip normal skinning for far bodies - NOT DONE, and why
`simple.frag` normalises the interpolated normal itself (line 61), so the CPU
`FastNormalize` could go for LOD bodies; but with the LOD in place the far bodies are 8-10 k
vertices and the normal's share of a vertex is 9 multiplies plus a Quake rsqrt - well
under 0.1 ms a frame in total. Not worth its branch. Leaving normals *unskinned* on far
bodies (bind-pose lighting) is visible as a body lit from the wrong side as it turns, so
that variant was not tried.

### 4. SSE2 kernels
`JointTransform` is four aligned columns; `AddWeighted`/`TransformPoint`/
`TransformDirection` are 4-wide (`SKINNING_SSE`, with the scalar branch for other
targets). objdump of `SkinInto`: 104 mulss + 79 addss -> 30 mulps + 25 addps.

### Also found: the replay was O(spatials x frames)
`perf2.data` (LOD build, a goal replay inside the window): `Match::ApplyReplayFrame` 20.6%
self time - it walked every spatial's frame buffer from the front on every put (~1,300
skeleton nodes x ~1,000 frames), and the replay window ran at 68 fps / 14.7 ms against
~200 fps in play. Now `std::lower_bound` (landed in c41eed7, CutsceneAudit consulted).
