### Rigging

**Arms and hands are broken on every model, in cutscenes and in gameplay.**

Measured with `skin_probe.py` (frame 16 of the lineup anim, hdg_2411), setting
one joint to identity at a time:

| tracks | worst edge stretch | edges past 2x |
|---|---|---|
| all | 14.9x | 791 |
| chest at identity | 14.9x | 791 |
| clavicles at identity | 14.1x | 761 |
| **shoulders at identity** | **2.5x** | **1** |

`left_shoulder` turns **82 degrees**, and linear blend skinning cannot hold a
joint through that: it collapses and drags the torso skin beside it. That is
the ballooning. Naming the joints that own the stretched edges points at the
chest, because chest-bound skin is what gets dragged; the cause is the
shoulder.

Native gameplay anims reach the same angles (090_decel peaks at 84.9 degrees),
which is why it shows in play as well as in cutscenes.

**Measured in absolute metres, and the import is fully exonerated.** Earlier
work argued this from ratios on 2.8 mm edges, which meant nothing. Redone
against PES itself, on `hdg_2411` posed by `pes_dml_ent_anth01_cmn01`:

- **The joints are exact.** Compare PES's own FK over the raw gani against GF's
  FK over the converted clip *on the converter's frame mapping*
  (`t = f * GF_FRAME_MS / PES_FRAME_MS`, 10 ms against 1/59.94 s - lining frame
  N against frame N is a trap that manufactures a fault). Hands, elbows and head
  agree to **0.0000 m** across the whole animation.
- **The weights are PES's own.** Its `boots.fmdl` ships 34,011 of 44,067
  vertices single-bound (77%); our import carries 19,449 of 26,957 (72%). We are
  not truncating anything - the rig really is that sparse.
- **The bind matches.** `retarget.PES_BIND` is GF's bind axis-permuted, Fox
  `(x, y, z)` to GF `(x, -z, y)`; both put shoulder-to-elbow 45 degrees below
  horizontal at 0.290 m. No mismatch.

**The tear, and its arithmetic.** Shoulder edges rest at a median 12.5 mm. Under
LBS the worst stretches by **+74.2 mm** - and by the same amount at frame 0, so
it is *pose-independent*: the arms sit about 50 degrees from bind in every frame
of every clip. The worst edges join vertices weighted `left_shoulder` 0.53 /
`chest` 0.47 against `left_shoulder` 0.98 / `chest` 0.02, 11 mm apart, each
0.18 m from the pivot. A 45-point weight jump over an 11 mm edge at a 0.18 m
lever through 0.9 rad gives 0.45 x 0.18 x 0.9 = 73 mm. That is the whole defect.

**Two fixes tried offline against that metric; neither works.**

- **Dual-quaternion skinning is worse**: +84.9 mm against LBS's +74.2. That
  settles what the earlier bad metric only guessed at - do not wire DQS again.
- **Smoothing the shoulder weights plateaus**: 2 rounds 51.4 mm, 4 rounds
  47.0, 8 rounds 41.0, 16 rounds 34.6. It never approaches zero, so a residual
  cause survives the weight gradient. Looked: it is not stray bindings. Every
  residual edge is in one 11 mm-wide band at the armpit - x about +-0.18,
  z 1.29 to 1.32 - joining vertices weighted `chest` 0.74 / `left_shoulder` 0.26
  against 0.50 / 0.50, mirrored on the right. A 24-point weight difference at a
  0.18 m lever through 0.9 rad is 39 mm, which is what is left. The seam tears
  because `chest` and `left_shoulder` rotate far apart and LBS interpolates
  transforms rather than rotations; no weighting removes that, it only moves it.

**Root cause: we collapse PES's deltoid bone.** `boots.fmdl` carries 25 bones,
and among them `dsk_deltoid_l` and `dsk_deltoid_r` hold **988 and 936 vertices**
- precisely the armpit band that tears. `retarget.py:307` folds both onto
`left_shoulder` / `right_shoulder`:

    "dsk_deltoid_l": "left_shoulder", "dsk_deltoid_r": "right_shoulder",

The obvious reading is that a deltoid is a half-angle helper - it turns by some
fraction of the upper arm so the shoulder skins smoothly - and that folding it
onto the arm gives those ~960 vertices per side the full angle. **Measured, and
that reading is wrong.** Synthesising the joint (same weights, the shoulder's
transform replaced by a slerp between chest and shoulder for the 896 left and
773 right vertices that carry both) makes the tear monotonically worse the less
it rotates: 0.50 gives +134.5 mm, 0.65 gives +115.6, 0.80 gives +96.8, against
+74.2 at 1.00, which is today's behaviour. Binding the band rigidly to a
half-angle joint is worse still, +97.7 mm, because it opens two fresh seams at
the band's own borders.

Those vertices want *more* of the shoulder, not less. So the fold is real and
the bone is real, but "half-angle helper" is not the mechanism, and a tenth
explanation is dead. If `dsk_deltoid` matters it is because PES drives it from
its own track or procedurally - note the track map only carries units for
shoulder, upperarm and forearm ((11,0), (11,2), (11,3)), so whatever moves the
deltoid is not in the data we read.

Note also that GF's `left_shoulder` *is* PES's `sk_upperarm_l` - the binds are
the same point - and GF already has `left_clavicle` for PES's `sk_shoulder_l`.
The deltoid is the one joint in that chain with nowhere to go.

**The hand half is fixed** (`11a2316`). It was never rigging: DBG's pack keeps
each player's forearms, hands and nineteen finger joints per side under
`Gloves/gNNNN` and the importer only ever read `Boots/`. dbg_2007 went from a
stump with a red cuff to a hand with fingers; 12,536 of its vertices now carry
finger weights where none did. hdg_2411 gained 2,268 vertices and 1,918 on
finger joints.

**The arm half is still open, and it is the armpit seam.** Re-measured on the
re-imported model, unchanged by the gloves because they add forearms rather than
armpit geometry: 216 of 14,241 shoulder edges stretch past 10 mm on a mesh whose
rest edges are 12.5 mm, 67 past 20 mm, worst 74.2 mm. That is a band, not a
sliver - it is visible.

Four candidates measured, none shippable:

| candidate | worst edge | edges over 10 mm |
|-----------|-----------|------------------|
| as imported | 74.2 mm | 216 |
| dual-quaternion skinning | 84.9 mm | - |
| neighbour smoothing, 16 rounds | 34.6 mm | - |
| half-angle deltoid | 96.8-134.5 mm | - |
| distance ramp across the armpit | 96.6 mm | **21** |

The ramp is the only one that helps the seam - a tenfold cut in torn edges - and
it makes the worst single edge worse, and the outliers survive one, two or three
rings of growth, so they are particular vertices rather than a boundary artefact.

**Settled by looking at it** (`f46266d`). `pose_render.py` skins offline the way
`humanoidbase.cpp` does and draws it with `ase_render` - no engine, no window.
Rendered at four frames of the lineup cutscene, including frame 16 which the
probe called worst, hdg_2411's shoulders are **smooth and round**. The 216 edges
stretching past 10 mm are spread over the pauldron's own small triangles and do
not read as a tear at any pose.

So the visible half of the complaint was the hands, and that was the Gloves
slot. The armpit seam is a number, not a defect.

And the ramp would have been a disaster: it cuts torn edges from 216 to 21 and
**shreds both shoulders into spikes**. That is the second time a metric here
pointed the wrong way - dual quaternions were the first - which is the whole
argument for the renderer existing. Judge a rig by looking at it.

### Picture

**Vertical banding on the rocks, adboards and clouds.** Reported on screen by
the owner, who traced it to the fringe fix (`2b6fbb0`, "Blur along an edge, not
across it").

Measured over 16 sampled seconds of the same fixture, column-to-column
alternation in the scenery band:

| edge blur | banding |
|---|---|
| off (`graphics_edge_blur_tolerance` 0) | 0.649 |
| selective (0.02, current) | 0.645 |
| flat average (100, the pre-fix behaviour) | 0.561 |

So the banding is **not caused by the blur**: turning it off entirely changes
nothing. It is not mipmapping either - rebuilt with mipmaps off, 0.645 against
0.649. What the old flat average did was smear it over, at the cost of the dark
fringe on every silhouette that `2b6fbb0` removed. Fixing the fringe unmasked
detail that was always there.

The source is the texture. `namek.png` is a 4096 atlas whose islands butt
straight against hard black gutters with no dilation, and the rock faces are
built from many small islands, so every seam is a dark edge; the pink islands
also carry painted strata. Bringing the smear back is not the answer.

Dilating the atlas gutters was tried and **did not help either**: bleeding each
island's colour eight pixels into the transparent gutter (8.4% of namek.png
filled, alpha untouched) measured 0.663 against 0.649 before. Reverted.

So four mechanisms are eliminated by measurement - the edge blur, its depth
test, mipmapping, and the atlas gutter. What is left is the texture's own
painted content: Namek's rock faces carry vertical strata, and the pre-fix flat
average was smearing the whole frame enough to hide them.

That makes it an art/filtering decision rather than a bug. If it should be
softer, the honest levers are a mild filter aimed at the scenery, or accepting
some smear back deliberately - not another hunt for a renderer fault. Do not
re-test the blur, mipmaps or the gutter.

Two candidate fixes were built and reverted for want of evidence: a
slope-aware depth test in `postprocess.frag` (no measurable benefit) and
dual-quaternion skinning (see Rigging).

### Measure

- **Pass trial above 80 percent - not met, and it is not the tactics.** Seven
  side-measurements across two fixtures. Extreme sliders (HDG's narrow,
  vertical, counter-attacking set against DBG, `passqual.sh mg1/mg4/mg5`):
  85/71, 76/70, 71/64, pooled 72.8%. Neutral sides carrying the stock
  886-character tactics, where the two imported teams carry 128
  (`passqual.sh n1/n2 3 4 1.6 620 6 norec`, FC Cataluna v FC Bavaria): 70/79
  and 73/68, pooled **72.5%**. Indistinguishable - so the tactical extremity I
  suspected is not the cause, and ~72.5% is the pass model itself.

  Where the eight points go: `[pass-fail]` puts about two thirds of failures on
  **interception** (2-7 a match), the rest on a failed trap (0-3) and the
  occasional ball out (0-1).

  **And it is not pass length either.** `gameplaytuning.hpp` calibrates the
  execution curve against real completion rates - 92% under ten metres, 85% at
  twenty, 70% at thirty, 55% at forty. The `[pass-dist]` bands from the neutral
  runs (4/11/8/4/1/3 and 2/14/14/11/3/3 over the <5, 5-10, 10-15, 15-25, 25-40,
  >=40 m edges) give a mean chosen length of **14.2 and 15.7 m**, which is
  ordinary football, and folding that distribution through the curve above
  predicts **86%**. We deliver 72.5%.

  So the selection is not choosing rash long balls - that was fixed - and the
  distance model is not the sink.

  **Part of it was arithmetic** (`9966319`). `_GetPassingOdds` priced the
  ball's flight to a mid-lane interception at `|intersect - origin| * u`,
  where `intersect` is already `u` of the way down the lane - so the flight
  was charged at u-squared of its real length, worst at u = 0.5, which is
  exactly where a man steps in. Marked lanes read quicker and safer than
  they were. Fixed, and re-measured on the same neutral fixture: per-side
  72/80, 74/90, 65/76 - **mean 76.2%** against 72.5%, two sides over the
  target and one at 90%. Variance between six-minute sides is wide (65 to
  90), so treat single sides with suspicion.

  About ten points remain against the 86% the curve predicts. There is a **13.5-point hole at short range**,
  and `[pass-fail]` says two thirds of it is interception. Either defenders
  intercept too easily inside fifteen metres, or `_GetPassingOdds` under-prices
  a marked lane there. That is the next thing to price, and it is worth more
  than the eight points to the target: it is most of the difference between this
  and real football.
- **Goal rate after the keeper retune - answered.** Same three trials: xG
  0.41/0.76/1.38 for 0/1/1 goals, pooled **2 goals from 2.55 xG**. Before the
  retune it was 3.1 xG for 1.6 goals - 52% converted; now conversion tracks xG,
  so `gameplay_keeper_sharpness` 0.66 did what it was meant to and the keeper is
  no longer the binding constraint.

  **Chance creation is, and it is not the shooting code.** Measured from the
`ODDS:` line the shot decision already prints (`elizacontroller.cpp:1296`),
against the `[balance]` shot counts from the same runs:

- **The trigger is not tight.** Over 2009 evaluations, the gate
  `(odds + U(0,0.5)) * appetite > 0.5` fires **72.6%** of the time it is
  reached. Median odds 0.515.
- **Nothing is lost downstream.** Grouping those per-frame evaluations into
  possessions gives 10-12 shooting episodes per six-minute match, 7-9 of them
  with a real opening, against 5, 9 and 10 shots actually taken. Openings
  become shots at roughly one for one.
- **So the shortfall is arrivals.** A player reaches a shooting position about
  1.7 times a minute. Everything after that point works.

That rules out the shot gate, the appetite tuning and the shot execution, and
points the whole question at build-up - how often the ball is worked into the
final third at all. Note the unit trap before comparing with the older 24.6
shots per match: that was measured over ten-minute matches, so it is 2.46 per
real minute against 0.83 now. Both numbers are per real minute of play, not per
90 of clock, because `match_duration_minutes` compresses the clock and not the
football.
