### Rigging

**Arms and hands are broken on every model, in cutscenes and in gameplay.**

Measured with `skin_probe.py` (frame 16 of the lineup anim, hdg_2411), setting
one joint to identity at a time:

| tracks | worst edge stretch | edges past 2x |
|---|---|---|
| all | 14.9x | 791 |
| chest at identity | 14.9x | 791 |
| clavicles at identity | 14.1x | 761 |
| **shoulders at identity** | **2.5x** | **1** |

`left_shoulder` turns **82 degrees**, and linear blend skinning cannot hold a
joint through that: it collapses and drags the torso skin beside it. That is
the ballooning. Naming the joints that own the stretched edges points at the
chest, because chest-bound skin is what gets dragged; the cause is the
shoulder.

Native gameplay anims reach the same angles (090_decel peaks at 84.9 degrees),
which is why it shows in play as well as in cutscenes.

**Next**: this is a skinning problem, not an import one. Dual-quaternion
skinning is the standard answer to exactly this collapse and `skinning.cpp`
already isolates the per-joint transform behind one interface, with its own
test suite. Do not re-open the import: PES's weights arrive intact.

Eight explanations were argued from source and killed - bind-pose mismatch,
quaternion axis conversion, finger joints stealing forearm weights, stale
offsets, animation smoothing, clavicle displacement, wrist tracks, and
proximity-guessed weights. Do not re-derive them.

**Celebrations snap players supine mid-air.** `SetChoreoPose` reads
`position.Get2D()`, discarding Z, so a choreography pose cannot lift an actor
off the ground - but the supine snap comes from the celebration clip's own root
motion via `e_FunctionType_Special` selection, not from choreography.
`match.cpp:1989-1994` documents the same failure mode being hit before.

### Picture

**Vertical banding on the rocks, adboards and clouds.** Reported on screen by
the owner, who traced it to the fringe fix (`2b6fbb0`, "Blur along an edge, not
across it").

Measured over 16 sampled seconds of the same fixture, column-to-column
alternation in the scenery band:

| edge blur | banding |
|---|---|
| off (`graphics_edge_blur_tolerance` 0) | 0.649 |
| selective (0.02, current) | 0.645 |
| flat average (100, the pre-fix behaviour) | 0.561 |

So the banding is **not caused by the blur**: turning it off entirely changes
nothing. It is not mipmapping either - rebuilt with mipmaps off, 0.645 against
0.649. What the old flat average did was smear it over, at the cost of the dark
fringe on every silhouette that `2b6fbb0` removed. Fixing the fringe unmasked
detail that was always there.

The source is the texture. `namek.png` is a 4096 atlas whose islands butt
straight against hard black gutters with no dilation, and the rock faces are
built from many small islands, so every seam is a dark edge; the pink islands
also carry painted strata. Bringing the smear back is not the answer.

**Next**: dilate atlas gutters at conversion time in `stadium_to_gf._texture_png`
- flood each island's edge colour outwards a few pixels so a seam samples rock
rather than black - and re-measure with the same metric. Do not re-test the
blur or mipmaps; both are eliminated above.

Two candidate fixes were built and reverted for want of evidence: a
slope-aware depth test in `postprocess.frag` (no measurable benefit) and
dual-quaternion skinning (see Rigging).

### Entrance

- **The pennant's own artwork is still wrong**, though it is now held and
  simulated (`6aa27af`). Two importer faults found while wiring it:
  - the flag's texture reads **mirrored** on screen;
  - the competition emblem is composited onto `prop000`, which on this ring is
    four scraps of 11 to 32 vertices, while the visible flag wears `prop001` -
    so the 4cc badge never lands on the thing anybody looks at, and
    `stadium_props.is_pennant_face` documents the opposite of what the data
    does.
- **Torn skydome backdrop - fixed** (`c5ddfb3`). The wedge and the blocky
  facets beside it were one material line: the converter wrote imported domes
  like walls (AMBIENT 0.588, SELFILLUM 0) where the engine's own sky.ase is
  AMBIENT 1, DIFFUSE 1, SELFILLUM 1. Sky mean RGB (54,125,50) with the wedge ->
  (78,181,65) clean.

  Two dead ends, recorded so they are not repeated:
  - **The backdrop's long edges are not shards.** 513 of 9,984 faces carry an
    edge over twenty times the mesh's median, 330 m against 3.89 m. Wiring
    `stretched_cut` into `stadium_to_gf` drops 0.28% of the stadium and *tears
    the landscape apart*: the ground is a few enormous flat polygons among fine
    rock detail, so the legitimate ones are exactly what the heuristic catches.
  - **It was not the constant normal's direction.** Flipping it changes the
    brightness and leaves the wedge identically shaped.

  Still owed: `st017` and `st031` have skies whose `MESH_NORMALS` an older
  `ase_slim` stripped (st002 kept its 1052). Today's slimmer keeps them, so a
  fresh import is right; existing installs want regenerating.
- **Stray PES competition assets.** Left alone deliberately - not committed, so
  they cost a user nothing.
- **Entrance camera collides with models - fixed** (`8090693`). Two faults, one
  symptom. `StadiumToken` read "the first st in the path", which is the one in
  "stadiums", so every ground asked for tracks called `stadi*`, got none, and
  was filmed with whatever shot sorted first. And entrance 020 ships no st017
  shot at all, so it fell back to st000's - a camera at another ground's tunnel
  mouth. A stadium's own shot is now preferred from any family; 001 and 011
  both carry st017.
